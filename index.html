I am a Ph.D. candidate in Sociology at Brown University, where I also earned an M.S. in Data Science. I study work, organizations, and technology. I am co-advised by Andrew Schrank and Han Zhang.

While a growing literature asks how AI shapes society, I ask the question in reverse: how do social relations, labor regimes, and organizational arrangements shape AI innovation in the first place? Through organizational ethnography across the AI innovation chain in the United States and China, I study how human contributions to AI are evaluated and differentiated: which are valued and recognized, and which are obscured and reclassified as machine performance.

In 2026, I was a Ph.D. Research Intern at Microsoft Research, mentored by Nancy Baym, where I studied human–AI coordination and workflow optimization.

I am on the 2026–27 academic job market.

Research

  • Book project: Human in / off the Loop: Organizing Expertise in Artificial Intelligence
    Why is the same human labor concealed as repetitive work in one frontier of AI yet foregrounded as expert demonstration in another? Drawing on 12 months of ethnography, working inside a frontier research institute and startups in Seattle and Hangzhou and data annotation firms across China, my dissertation compares the development of large language models in the United States with embodied robotics in China, the leading domains of AI innovation in each country. I argue that the value of human input depends on how settled a technology is and how much legitimacy it enjoys among scientists and investors, and that the U.S.–China AI race locks these differences into distinct national pathways.
  • AI and the future of knowledge work
    At Microsoft Research, I combined 40 work-shadowing sessions with six teams with individual- and team-level telemetry on AI use. I develop the concept of asymmetric scalability: AI scales technical tasks faster than the social coordination those tasks require. Ongoing papers examine the temporality of AI-assisted work and how coordination costs and productivity shift within teams as AI use grows.
  • Technology, cultural politics, and computational methods
    I treat digital artifacts, from platform metrics and social media posts to LLM outputs, as evidence for long-standing debates about representation, status, and differentiation. This includes work on platformized governance of contention in China, on how LLMs reason about race, gender, and class, and on the epistemology of AI-assisted qualitative analysis.
